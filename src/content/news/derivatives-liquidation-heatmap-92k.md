The cryptocurrency derivatives landscape is exhibiting conditions conducive to an aggressive short squeeze, according to consolidated order book and liquidation cluster data across global digital asset exchanges.

As Bitcoin stabilizes near the upper boundary of its current range, proprietary trading desks note that algorithmic hedge funds and macro short sellers have accumulated leveraged positions defending the \$90,000–\$92,000 threshold.

## Liquidation Densities and Order Flow Imbalance

According to data audited by Coinglass, a break above **\$91,500** would trigger initial liquidations of approximately \$640 million in short positions. Should upward momentum sustain through **\$92,800**, cumulative automated buy-to-cover market orders would exceed **\$1.84 billion**.

These forced liquidations function as an accelerant, injecting non-discretionary market buy orders into a market already characterized by thin public ask depth on spot exchanges.

## Institutional Options Positioning Targets \$100K

On institutional options exchange Deribit, trading volume has heavily favored out-of-the-money call options. The **\$100,000 strike** expiring at the end of the upcoming quarter maintains the single largest concentration of open contracts, representing over \$2.4 billion in notional value.

Market makers who have sold these call options are required to dynamically hedge their exposure by purchasing spot Bitcoin as price ascends, creating a reinforcing mechanism known in quantitative finance as a "gamma squeeze."

Traders and risk managers are advised to exercise discipline around position sizing, as high liquidation volatility often produces abrupt whipsaws designed to flush both over-leveraged longs and shorts before decisive trend continuation.
